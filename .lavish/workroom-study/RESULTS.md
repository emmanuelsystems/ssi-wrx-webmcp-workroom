# Pstack-style SSI-WRX screen — results

## Result

The neutral guidance and pstack-derived guidance tied at 10/10 in the blinded judgment. This screen does not demonstrate that the added pstack guidance improves SSI-WRX analysis.

North was the neutral condition. West was the pstack-derived condition. The judge did not receive this mapping.

The judge found West marginally sharper about the decision and observable pass condition, while North was marginally more concise. It found neither difference large enough to change a score.

## Root review

I agree with the tie. Both responses correctly distinguished implemented behavior, intended product boundaries, historical receipts, missing governance features, and unproved Astra work. Both selected the four-card reconstruction screen and limited what a pass would establish.

The pstack-derived condition visibly changed the response shape: it restated the decision first and added that the packet lacked end-to-end browser validation across all relevant paths. Those are useful behaviors, but this run does not show a meaningful outcome advantage.

## What this tells us

- The neutral baseline was already strong enough to saturate the 0–2 rubric.
- The source packet made the central uncertainty and recommended action unusually explicit, reducing room for the working guide to help.
- A single run per condition cannot establish repeatability.
- The screen evaluated a small adapted guide, not the full pstack plugin or its multi-model orchestration.
- The ephemeral runs showed both candidates reading `INPUT.md`; they did not retain complete local transcripts suitable for a deeper instruction-loading audit.

## Recommendation

Do not promote the pstack-derived guide based on this tie. Keep the practices as useful heuristics and test them on a harder behavioral task where the baseline can fail: conflicting sources, an ambiguous authority boundary, or a disposable prototype whose observed behavior settles a real design choice.

## September 10 coherence-protocol update

David shared two new reference artifacts in Slack and directed the team to use them together:

- **COHERENCE CHECK v2.5** is a read-only alignment audit. It reconstructs accessible context, classifies coverage and artifact authority, separates confirmed intent from inference, identifies missing warrants and rival readings, recommends one next step, and ends with an `ALIGNMENT_RECORD` in `PENDING_CONFIRMATION` state.
- **COHERENCE CONTINUATION v1.1** begins from an applicable confirmed record, connects new developments to the established understanding, and routes the next step without repeating the complete audit. It preserves separate states for alignment confirmation, step clearance, and execution request.

These files strengthen the SSI-WRX authority model and address a weakness in the first pstack screen: the screen used a prepared source packet but did not begin from a confirmed alignment record with explicit artifact states and lineage.

They should not be inserted into the pstack candidates' visible context. Doing so would disclose the assessment structure, change the task, and invalidate the blind comparison. The better sequence is:

1. Explicitly invoke COHERENCE CHECK v2.5 on the bounded SSI-WRX and pstack workstream.
2. Let Emmanuel confirm or correct the resulting alignment record.
3. Use COHERENCE CONTINUATION v1.1 to decide whether the synthetic four-card dry run or a human-labeled real packet is the next cleared step.
4. Keep pstack as the method for blinding, cheap prototyping, and verification inside that aligned scope.

Neither file is active merely because it was reviewed. Their own activation rules require the user to paste or explicitly invoke the applicable protocol.
